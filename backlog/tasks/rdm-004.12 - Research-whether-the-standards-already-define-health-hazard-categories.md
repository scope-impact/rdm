## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
## Question

Does an existing standard supply a hazard-category taxonomy for health/safety harms in software, structured the way STRIDE is — a closed set generated by negating a property set — or must one be adapted?

This informs how the schema models hazard classification: one level or two, closed vocabulary or open, and whether a sub-taxonomy hangs off a domain. It does **not** decide the taxonomy content, which is an `RMP-001` revision and out of scope for this map.

## What is already established

- **`ISO-82304-2:4.1-Q7` requires "Hazard categories defined and documented"** and supplies no list. `4.1-Q6` separately requires a "structured hazard identification approach". So the obligation exists and the content is the organisation to provide.
- **`RMP-001` already declares three** (lines 98-106): Health/Safety, Data/Privacy, Operations, tagged `[[ISO-82304-2:4.1-Q7]]`, and `rdm gap` reports the clause covered. Two defects in that declaration: its examples mix the axes (Health/Safety lists "User harm" alongside "incorrect health advice" and "missed alerts" — a harm and two origins), and it claims "each elaborated in the risk clusters, RC-* — see WI-1", which does not resolve: WI-1 defines four clusters against three categories and Health/Safety had no cluster at all until the wallet added `RC-SAFETY`.
- **STRIDE works because each member negates a security property** (Spoofing/authentication, Tampering/integrity, Repudiation/non-repudiation, Information Disclosure/confidentiality, Denial of Service/availability, Elevation of Privilege/authorization), which closes the set by construction.
- **A candidate adaptation exists but is not a citation.** Negating the Five Rights of Clinical Decision Support gives: Inaccurate (right information), Inapplicable (right person), Unintelligible (right format), Misrouted (right channel), Untimely (right time). Tested against the wallet five scored safety risks it fits 5 of 5, with `Unintelligible` unused — which correctly flags that comprehension risk is unassessed, and the "Not yet assessed" list does contain translation fidelity.

## What to investigate

1. **ISO 14971:2019 Annex C** — particularly its hazard categories and the information-hazards material. Is the list a prompt list for identification, or a filing taxonomy for classification? Does it have a generative structure or is it enumerative?
2. **IEC 62366-1** (usability engineering / use error) — does it categorise use errors in a way that would serve as the health analogue, and how does it relate to hazard categories?
3. **ISO/TS 82304-2** itself — does the standard behind `4.1-Q7` suggest or imply categories anywhere, or genuinely leave them open?
4. **Clinical decision support literature** — is the Five Rights framing (Osheroff et al.) actually used as a *safety* taxonomy anywhere, or only as a design heuristic? If the former, cite where.
5. Any other domain taxonomy with STRIDE structure for patient-safety or health-software hazards.

For each: whether it is closed or open, whether it is generated from a property set, its size, and whether it is intended for classification or identification.

## Constraints

Primary sources. Where a standard text is not reachable, say so explicitly and mark the resulting claim as inference rather than citation — as `RDM-004.05` did for the ReqIF specification prose. Do **not** propose a taxonomy for adoption; report what exists and what it costs.

## The one finding verified locally, and it corrects the ticket

**`ISO-82304-2:4.1-Q7` is a row in a locally authored checklist, not a clause of the standard.** Verified directly: `documentation/checklist/iso_checklist.txt` was added in a single commit (`d781561 Add RDM tooling and gap analysis infrastructure`), rdm ships **no** 82304-2 checklist at all (it ships 14971, 62304, FDA and part-11 checklists only), and neighbouring rows are plainly local editorial — `4.1-Q5 Patient safety principles applied for wellness apps` is not standard-clause language.

So `rdm gap` reporting `4.1-Q7` covered means RMP-001's three categories satisfy **a row we wrote ourselves**. The obligation this ticket rested on has no verified external anchor. That does not make the categories wrong — it makes the schema free to choose, rather than constrained by a standard.

## What prior art exists

**Nothing with STRIDE generative structure serves as a health-hazard *category* taxonomy.** `[SRCH]` The distinction the ticket asked for turns out to be the whole answer:

- Everything **closed and generative** is a per-element **identification** prompt, not a filing bin: SHARD/HAZOP (5 guide words, per data flow), STPA unsafe control actions (4 types, per control action), LINDDUN (7 privacy threats — the one successful replication of negating a property set, and it is privacy not safety).
- Everything shaped like a **filing** taxonomy is open, empirically grown, and built for post-market incident reporting: WHO ICPS, Magrabi 32 preferred terms, NCC MERP, AHRQ Health IT Hazard Manager. A systematic review counted **77** such classifications.
- **There is essentially no prior art for a short closed set used to file pre-market design risks** — which is exactly what RMP-001 three categories are.

## Two findings that cut against this ticket and against the candidate

**IEC 62366:2007 had a generative use-error taxonomy — slip / lapse / mistake — and 62366-1:2015 removed it** `[SRCH]`, reportedly because the class boundaries were not reliably discriminable. If that holds on verification it is the best available price tag on a negation-derived taxonomy: a standards committee tried this exact move and reversed it.

**The Five Rights is a design heuristic only** `[SRCH]`. No peer-reviewed source publishes its negation as a safety taxonomy; CDS-safety taxonomies classify *causes*, not harms. The one real negate-the-rights precedent in patient safety is NCC MERP, derived from the *nursing* five rights of medication administration, not from Osheroff. So the "Five Wrongs" candidate has no published precedent and would be ours entirely.

**Also: "the standards supply no list" was too strong for ISO 14971:2019** `[SRCH]` — it reportedly supplies four category headings (energy, biological and chemical, operational, information). Two are permanently empty for a wellness app, and all four are *sources of harm*, so filing harms under them would reproduce the axis-mixing defect this work is trying to remove.

## What this means for the dependent decisions

The taxonomy question is not "which existing list do we adopt" — no adoptable list was found. It is: **do we want an identification instrument or a filing instrument?** Those have different shapes, and the estate currently conflates them. A closed generative set (like the Five Wrongs) is the shape of an identification prompt; a coverage-checkable filing taxonomy is the shape nobody builds closed. Carried into `documentation` DOC-9.06 and DOC-9.07.

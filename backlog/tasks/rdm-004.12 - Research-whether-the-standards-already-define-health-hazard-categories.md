## Question

Does an existing standard supply a hazard-category taxonomy for health/safety harms in software, structured the way STRIDE is — a closed set generated by negating a property set — or must one be adapted?

This informs how the schema models hazard classification: one level or two, closed vocabulary or open, and whether a sub-taxonomy hangs off a domain. It does **not** decide the taxonomy content, which is an `RMP-001` revision and out of scope for this map.

## What is already established

- **`ISO-82304-2:4.1-Q7` requires "Hazard categories defined and documented"** and supplies no list. `4.1-Q6` separately requires a "structured hazard identification approach". So the obligation exists and the content is the organisation to provide.
- **`RMP-001` already declares three** (lines 98-106): Health/Safety, Data/Privacy, Operations, tagged `[[ISO-82304-2:4.1-Q7]]`, and `rdm gap` reports the clause covered. Two defects in that declaration: its examples mix the axes (Health/Safety lists "User harm" alongside "incorrect health advice" and "missed alerts" — a harm and two origins), and it claims "each elaborated in the risk clusters, RC-* — see WI-1", which does not resolve: WI-1 defines four clusters against three categories and Health/Safety had no cluster at all until the wallet added `RC-SAFETY`.
- **STRIDE works because each member negates a security property** (Spoofing/authentication, Tampering/integrity, Repudiation/non-repudiation, Information Disclosure/confidentiality, Denial of Service/availability, Elevation of Privilege/authorization), which closes the set by construction.
- **A candidate adaptation exists but is not a citation.** Negating the Five Rights of Clinical Decision Support gives: Inaccurate (right information), Inapplicable (right person), Unintelligible (right format), Misrouted (right channel), Untimely (right time). Tested against the wallet five scored safety risks it fits 5 of 5, with `Unintelligible` unused — which correctly flags that comprehension risk is unassessed, and the "Not yet assessed" list does contain translation fidelity.

## What to investigate

1. **ISO 14971:2019 Annex C** — particularly its hazard categories and the information-hazards material. Is the list a prompt list for identification, or a filing taxonomy for classification? Does it have a generative structure or is it enumerative?
2. **IEC 62366-1** (usability engineering / use error) — does it categorise use errors in a way that would serve as the health analogue, and how does it relate to hazard categories?
3. **ISO/TS 82304-2** itself — does the standard behind `4.1-Q7` suggest or imply categories anywhere, or genuinely leave them open?
4. **Clinical decision support literature** — is the Five Rights framing (Osheroff et al.) actually used as a *safety* taxonomy anywhere, or only as a design heuristic? If the former, cite where.
5. Any other domain taxonomy with STRIDE structure for patient-safety or health-software hazards.

For each: whether it is closed or open, whether it is generated from a property set, its size, and whether it is intended for classification or identification.

## Constraints

Primary sources. Where a standard text is not reachable, say so explicitly and mark the resulting claim as inference rather than citation — as `RDM-004.05` did for the ReqIF specification prose. Do **not** propose a taxonomy for adoption; report what exists and what it costs.
